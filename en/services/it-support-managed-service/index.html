What We Solve

  • Not yet ready to hire full-time IT staff, but still need routine handling
  • IT problems get patched up haphazardly by staff who aren't in that field
  • No system documentation — if the person leaves, nobody knows how it works
  • Need a partner who can be called in an emergency and comes on site

Services We Handle

Remote & On-site IT Support

Remote assistance for issues that can be resolved from a distance, and on-site visits for hardware, networking, or incidents that need to be handled in person. You have a single line to report to, instead of finding a new technician every time something breaks.

Network & Server Support

Routine handling and repairs for your network devices and servers, from connectivity issues to services that have stopped. Key configurations are recorded so the next fix is faster.

IT Troubleshooting

Tracing the root cause when a device, application, or connection isn't behaving normally, then fixing it through to completion. The steps and cause are recorded so the same problem doesn't repeat unnoticed.

Preventive Maintenance

Scheduled checks on servers, networks, and supporting devices before anything breaks — updates, disk capacity, data backups, and hardware condition. The goal is fewer sudden disruptions during working hours.

Infrastructure Monitoring

Monitoring the condition of your servers, network, and critical services from one place, so signs of trouble are visible before they affect users. Notifications go out when something needs attention.

IT Maintenance

Periodic upkeep of your entire IT estate: system updates, cleanup, replacement of aging components, and tidying up documentation. This work runs on a schedule, not only once something is already wrong.

Managed IT Services

We handle your day-to-day IT management under a scope and response target agreed on upfront, including periodic reports. A good fit if you want IT costs that can be budgeted, not surprise costs when something breaks.

IT Consultation

Technical input when you need to decide on a hardware purchase, a system change, or an infrastructure improvement. You get an honest read on the trade-offs of each option before spending anything.

How We Do It

01 IT asset assessment & inventory

We document your devices, servers, network, accounts, and licenses along with their condition, then note the points most prone to trouble. The result becomes the basis for a realistic service scope.

02 Scope & SLA agreement

We put in writing what's covered, response time targets per severity level, maintenance schedule, and the monthly cost before the service begins.

03 Onboarding, documentation & monitoring setup

We tidy up system documentation, administrator accounts, and procedures, then set up monitoring with alert thresholds so infrastructure health is tracked from day one.

04 Ongoing support + periodic reporting

Our team responds to reports and alerts per the SLA, carries out routine maintenance, and sends periodic reports covering the work done and improvement recommendations.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between a managed service and calling a technician?

Calling a technician is reactive: you get in touch once something is already broken, and the cost comes up every time it happens. A managed service is ongoing: routine maintenance, monitoring, and documentation happen continuously under a scope and cost agreed on upfront, so some problems are caught before they disrupt operations. The two can be combined — many clients use a monthly contract for routine handling and still call us for anything outside that scope.

How fast is the response time?

Response time targets are set at the start of the contract and vary by severity. Issues that halt operations are handled first and are usually responded to the same business day, while lighter requests are scheduled afterward. We put the exact figures in writing in the service agreement so you know what to expect, not just a verbal promise.

Is there a minimum contract length?

Managed services generally run on a recurring contract, monthly or annual for example, because the benefits of routine maintenance and monitoring only show up when they run continuously. We discuss the minimum length based on your situation, and for one-off work such as troubleshooting or an audit, no contract is needed.
