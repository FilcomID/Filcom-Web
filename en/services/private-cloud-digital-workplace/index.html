What We Solve

  • Cloud storage subscription costs keep rising as more users are added
  • Company data is stored in employees' personal accounts
  • You need email on your own domain, not a personal Gmail/Yahoo address
  • Your team needs secure access to files from outside the office
  • You want data to stay on your own server for confidentiality reasons

Services We Handle

Private Cloud Deployment

We build a cloud service on your own server, complete with access controls, encrypted connections, and scheduled backups. You retain full control over your data and who can access it.

Cloud Storage

Centralized file storage accessible to your entire team via browser or desktop app. Capacity follows your server's disk, so there's no per-gigabyte bill that grows every month.

File Sharing & Collaboration

Folder sharing between divisions, password-protected share links with expiry dates, and shared document editing. Version history is kept so overwritten files can still be restored.

Self-Hosted Cloud

Every service runs on your own server, not a third party's. Suitable when confidentiality needs or regulations require that data never leave your company environment.

Nextcloud Deployment

Installation and configuration of Nextcloud, including connecting it to your existing login accounts, calendar and contacts apps, and sync clients on computers and phones. We also set baseline security and a backup pattern.

Mail Server

Email on your own company domain, complete with spam filtering and SPF, DKIM, and DMARC settings so your email is trusted by receiving servers. Accessible via webmail or standard email apps.

Web & Application Hosting

Running websites or internal applications on your own server, with a domain, HTTPS certificate, and separation between services. Useful for applications you don't want placed on rented services.

Remote Access

Access to files and applications from outside the office via encrypted connections, VPN, or restricted links. We set it up so only authorized people and devices can get in.

Cloud Migration & Management

Moving data from Google Drive, OneDrive, or other services to your own cloud, followed by routine management such as updates, capacity monitoring, and backup recovery tests.

How We Do It

01 Needs & capacity assessment

We document the number of users, file volume, email needs, and applicable confidentiality rules, then estimate the appropriate server capacity and backup pattern.

02 Server setup & installation (Nextcloud/mail)

The server is prepared, then Nextcloud and the mail server are installed along with baseline security, an HTTPS certificate, and administrator accounts.

03 Data migration & domain configuration

Data from your old service is migrated, the domain is pointed over, and SPF, DKIM, and DMARC settings are configured so email and cloud access run under your own domain.

04 Testing, brief training & handover

We test access from inside and outside the office, test backup recovery, provide brief training for your team, then hand over configuration documentation and administrator accounts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is private cloud cheaper than Google Workspace?

It depends on the number of users and how long the system is used. Private cloud requires an upfront cost for the server and setup, but after that there's no monthly per-user bill. The more accounts and the longer it's used, the bigger the difference usually becomes. For very small teams, or those who need many of Google's built-in features, a subscription service may be more practical. We help you work out the comparison based on your situation before you decide.

Where is the data stored?

On a server you designate, whether a physical server at your office or a rented server at a data center of your choice. The data isn't entrusted to a third-party account. You receive full administrator access along with documentation of the storage location and backups, so you always know where your files are.

What happens if the server goes down?

We set up scheduled backups to a separate location so your data stays safe even if the main hardware has issues. For needs that can't afford long downtime, options such as a backup server, UPS, or a secondary internet line are available. When an outage occurs, the system is restored from the latest backup, with a reasonable recovery time target agreed on in advance based on your situation.
